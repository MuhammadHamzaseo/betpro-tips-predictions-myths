Every cricket season brings a flood of tip sellers. "Today's sure-shot session." "100% fixed match, PSL final." "VIP BetPro tips, 95% accuracy." They advertise in WhatsApp groups, Telegram channels, TikTok comments and Facebook pages, often with screenshots of huge wins. Many Pakistani BetPro users wonder whether any of these are real, and some pay for them after a losing streak, hoping for a shortcut back.

This guide explains honestly why paid tips almost never work, how tip sellers make money even when their tips lose, how "fixed match" scams operate, and how to judge any tipster using simple tests. It doesn't publish tips or predictions, and it won't tell you who will win a match. What it will do is help you protect your money, and your peace of mind, from one of the most common and costly traps in the betting world.

The Short Answer

Nobody can sell you guaranteed cricket results. Markets on BetPro already reflect a huge amount of information from thousands of users, and outcomes in cricket, especially T20, are genuinely uncertain. Tipsters who claim high accuracy usually rely on selective screenshots, sending different tips to different people, or simply luck over a short period. "Fixed match" sellers are running scams. If a tipster really had an edge, they'd use it quietly rather than sell it to strangers for a few thousand rupees.

Why Predicting Cricket Is So Hard

  • Small margins decide results. A dropped catch, a no-ball or one big over can flip a T20 match.
  • The toss and conditions matter, and they're known only shortly before the match.
  • Form is noisy. Players and teams go through streaks that don't last.
  • Markets react fast. By the time a tipster posts, the price usually already reflects the information behind the tip.
  • Sessions are even harder. Predicting whether a team scores 45 or 46 in six overs is close to a coin flip, and the one-run gap means close calls often lose both ways.

How Tip Sellers Make Money When Their Tips Lose

1. Subscription Fees

The seller earns from your payment, not from the result. Whether the tip wins or loses, the fee is already in their wallet, and a losing tip simply becomes a reason to sell you the next one.

2. Split Tips

A seller sends "Team A" to half their subscribers and "Team B" to the other half. Whichever team wins, half the group sees a correct tip and some of them pay again. Repeat this across several matches and a small group will see a "perfect record" by chance.

3. Selective Screenshots

Winning tips are posted proudly with celebration messages; losing tips are deleted, edited or never mentioned again. A channel's visible record can therefore look far better than its real one.

4. Referral Commissions

Some tipsters also act as agents for a desk or bookmaker and earn a share of what their followers lose. Losing tips can actually earn them more.

5. Upselling "VIP" and "Fixed" Tips

Free tips build an audience. Paid "VIP" tips cost more. "Fixed match" tips cost the most. Each step extracts more money from the most hopeful followers.

"Fixed Match" Scams Explained

Match-fixing is a serious crime and a real concern in cricket's history, which is exactly why "fixed match" scams sound believable. Here's how they typically work:

  1. A seller claims to have "inside contacts" who know the result of an upcoming match or session.
  2. They demand an upfront payment, sometimes a large one, and promise a refund if the tip loses.
  3. Some also ask you to bet a large amount so that you can "share the profit" with them.
  4. If the tip wins by chance, they demand a share of your winnings and use your story to recruit others.
  5. If it loses, the "refund" never arrives, or they claim the fix "was cancelled" and offer another one for a new fee.

Genuine inside information on fixed matches, if it existed, would not be sold in public Telegram channels. Treat every "fixed match" offer as a scam, without exception.

How to Judge Any Tipster

If you still want to evaluate a tipster, use tests that can't be faked easily:

  1. Full record, not highlights: ask for every tip with dates, odds and stakes, including losses. A seller who refuses has something to hide.
  2. Tips published before the event: tips must be timestamped before the match starts, in a public place that can't be edited.
  3. Odds included: "Pakistan to win" means little without the odds. Backing favourites at short odds can have a high win rate and still lose money.
  4. Long sample: a few weeks proves nothing. Hundreds of tips over a long period are needed to separate skill from luck.
  5. No guarantees: anyone promising certainty, refunds or "95% accuracy" is selling a fantasy.
  6. No conflicts: ask whether they earn commission from a desk or bookmaker.

In practice, almost no paid tipster in the BetPro market passes these tests, and most refuse to even try. That refusal is itself the answer to whether their tips are worth buying.

Win Rate vs Profit: A Simple Example

Suppose a tipster claims a 70% win rate, and their tips are mostly favourites at odds of 1.30. If you stake PKR 1,000 on each of 10 tips, you'd win 7 and lose 3.

  • 7 wins × PKR 300 profit = PKR 2,100.
  • 3 losses × PKR 1,000 = −PKR 3,000.
  • Net result: −PKR 900, before commission.

A high win rate can still lose money. That's why "accuracy" claims without odds are meaningless. At odds of 1.30, a tipster needs to win about 77% of the time just to break even.

Better Alternatives to Paid Tips

  • Learn the markets: understand back, lay, liability, sessions, commission and settlement rules properly.
  • Bet less, not smarter: fewer, smaller bets reduce losses more reliably than any tip.
  • Keep a record of your own bets and results, so you can see what's really happening rather than what you remember.
  • Enjoy cricket without betting on some matches. Many people find they enjoy those matches more, not less.

The Maths of "Lucky" Tipsters

It's easy to underestimate how often luck produces an impressive-looking record. Imagine 1,000 people each making ten predictions on matches that are close to 50/50. By pure chance, around one person in a thousand would be expected to get all ten right. That person could honestly say "ten out of ten" and look like a genius, without any skill at all.

Now imagine a single tip seller running several channels, or splitting subscribers into groups with different tips. They can manufacture that lucky streak on purpose. The subscribers who happened to receive the winning tips see a perfect record and pay for more. The others quietly leave, and the seller starts again with new followers.

The lesson: a short run of correct tips tells you almost nothing. Without a long, complete and public record, there's no way to tell skill from luck, and in betting markets luck is by far the more common explanation.

Worked Scenario: The "VIP Channel"

This scenario is illustrative, based on patterns users commonly describe. Hassan joins a free Telegram channel after a bad week on BetPro. The channel posts three free tips; two of them win. Screenshots of other members' wins fill the chat. The admin announces a VIP group with "95% accuracy" for PKR 3,000 a week.

Hassan joins. Over the next week, the VIP tips win some and lose some, roughly what you'd expect by chance. When he asks about the losses, the admin says those were "risky extra tips" and offers a "confirmed fixed session" for PKR 10,000, with a refund promised if it loses. Hassan pays. The session loses. The refund never comes, and Hassan is removed from the group.

Hassan's losses came from three places: the VIP fee, the "fixed session" fee, and the bets he placed following the tips. None of it was necessary. The first warning sign, a paid promise of accuracy, was enough to walk away.

Red Flags in Tip Channels

  • Claims of "sure shot", "confirmed", "fixed" or "100%".
  • Win screenshots with no timestamps or with cropped details.
  • Deleted or edited messages after losing tips.
  • Pressure to pay quickly "before the toss" or "before spots run out".
  • Refund promises that are never honoured.
  • Requests to deposit with a specific dealer through their link.
  • Admins who remove members who ask questions.

Tips and Chasing Losses

Tip sellers deliberately target people who are losing. After a bad run, a "sure shot" sounds like a way to get back to even. That's exactly when buying tips is most dangerous: you're paying extra, betting more, and making decisions under stress. If you notice yourself looking for tips because you want to win back losses, that's a sign to stop betting for a while, not to find a better tipster.

What Knowledge Can Actually Do for You

Understanding cricket and markets won't let you predict results reliably, but it does help in real ways:

  • You'll avoid bets you don't understand, like lays with huge liability.
  • You'll read session lines correctly and avoid the one-run-gap surprise.
  • You'll know how rain, ties and reduced overs affect settlement.
  • You'll recognise when a price has already moved on news you just heard.
  • You'll be much harder for tip sellers and scammers to fool.

That's the honest value of knowledge: fewer mistakes and fewer scams, not guaranteed winnings.

Where Tip Sellers Find Their Customers

  • TikTok and Instagram accounts posting win screenshots with a contact number in the bio.

Notice that none of these channels publish complete, verifiable records. That's not an accident.

Understanding Why Markets Are Hard to Beat

On an exchange like BetPro, prices are set by the combined money and opinions of many users. When news breaks, such as a team change, the toss result or a key player's injury, prices adjust quickly as users react. A tip that arrives in your WhatsApp is almost always based on information that's already public and already reflected in the odds. To make money consistently, you'd need information or judgement that's better than the market's, and you'd need it repeatedly, over hundreds of bets. That's extremely rare, and people who genuinely have it don't need to sell it in public groups.

Common Myths About Tips

"The Tipster Must Know Because He Has Contacts"

Claims of contacts in teams, dressing rooms or "the betting mafia" are impossible to check and are a classic persuasion technique.

"Paying More Gets Better Tips"

Higher prices usually signal a bigger scam, not better information. The most expensive offers are typically the "fixed" ones.

"The Refund Guarantee Means No Risk"

Refunds are rarely honoured. Even when a small refund is paid, it's often in the form of "free tips" rather than money.

"A Winning Tip Proves the Next One Will Win"

Each match is a separate uncertain event. A correct tip yesterday says very little about today's match.

"AI Predictions Are Different"

Some sellers now claim their tips come from "AI", "software" or "data models". The same tests apply: a full, timestamped public record with odds over a long period. Labels don't make predictions reliable.

If You've Already Paid for Tips

  1. Stop paying for further tips, "VIP" upgrades or "fixed" matches.
  2. Don't chase the refund with another payment. Sellers often ask for a "processing fee" to release a refund.
  3. Keep evidence: screenshots of the channel, the seller's number and your payment receipts.
  4. Report the wallet or account you paid to Easypaisa, JazzCash or your bank.
  5. Leave the group and block the seller, so you're not drawn back in.
  6. Review your betting: if you bought tips to recover losses, take a break before betting again.

Session Tips: Why They're the Worst Value

Session tips, like "Pakistan 6 over Yes 48", are especially popular with sellers because sessions settle quickly and feel predictable. They aren't. Early overs depend on the first few balls, the bowlers chosen, the pitch and luck. The line already reflects what the market expects. On top of that, the gap between No and Yes means a score landing exactly between them loses for both sides. A seller who sends a session tip is giving you a coin flip with a small disadvantage built in, and charging you for it.

Keeping a Personal Betting Record Instead

The most useful "tips" you'll ever get come from your own record. For every bet, note the date, match, market, odds, stake and result. After a month, look for patterns:

  • Which markets have cost you the most?
  • Did late-night bets or bets after a loss do worse?
  • How much did you spend on sessions versus match odds?
  • What was your total result after commission?

Many people discover that a handful of impulsive bets caused most of their losses. Cutting those out does more for your balance than any tipster ever could.

Helping Someone Who Keeps Buying Tips

If a friend or relative keeps paying for tips, they're probably chasing losses and hoping for a shortcut. Criticism rarely helps. Instead, show them the win-rate-versus-odds maths, point out that the seller earns regardless of results, and suggest they track every tip for a month without betting on it. Seeing the real record usually changes minds faster than any argument. If their betting is causing debt or stress, gently encourage them to take a break and talk to someone.

A tipster who picks outsiders can have a low win rate and look terrible, even if the results are no worse. Accuracy only means something alongside odds, and even then only over a large number of tips.

Why Real Experts Rarely Sell Tips

Think about it from the seller's side. If someone could reliably make money from cricket betting, the rational thing would be to bet quietly with their own money, not to share their edge with thousands of strangers, which would move prices and destroy it. Selling tips is attractive precisely because it earns money without needing to be right. That's the business model, and it's why the tip market is so full of noise.

A Healthier Way to Enjoy Predictions

Predicting matches can be fun without money involved. Many friend groups run free prediction games: guessing winners, top scorers or totals for bragging rights. You get the enjoyment of analysis and debate without the financial risk, and nobody profits from your hopes. If you find that predictions only feel exciting when money is on the line, that's worth noticing.

How Tip Scams Use Emotions

Tip sellers are skilled at reading emotions, and they design their messages around a few feelings:

  • Hope: "This is the one that will fix your week."
  • Fear of missing out: "Only 10 VIP spots left before the toss."
  • Belonging: busy groups full of celebration make you feel part of a winning team.
  • Trust: friendly admins, religious greetings and personal attention.
  • Shame: after a loss, they suggest you "didn't follow the plan properly".

Recognising these tactics makes them far less powerful. When a message makes you feel urgent, hopeful or ashamed, that's the moment to slow down, not to pay.

Tips During Big Tournaments

PSL, IPL, Asia Cup and ICC events are peak season for tip sellers. New channels appear overnight, old ones rename themselves after a bad run, and "fixed match" offers multiply before knockouts and finals. During these weeks:

  • Ignore any channel or group that appeared only when the tournament started.
  • Be extra suspicious of "final special" or "semi-final confirmed" offers.
  • Don't join groups shared in comment sections under highlights.
  • Keep your own tournament budget and stick to it, tips or no tips.

Big matches feel important, but that doesn't make them more predictable. If anything, pressure makes outcomes less certain.

Signs Betting Is Becoming a Problem

  • Buying tips to recover losses.
  • Spending more than you planned, or topping up mid-month.
  • Borrowing, or using money meant for bills or family.
  • Hiding betting or tip purchases from people close to you.
  • Feeling restless or irritable when you can't bet.

If this sounds familiar, take a break and ask your desk to close your ID. Talking to someone you trust, or to a doctor or counsellor, can help.

A Simple Experiment You Can Try

If you're tempted by a tip channel, try this before paying anything. For one month, write down every free tip the channel posts, with the time it was posted, the market and the odds at that moment. Don't bet on any of them. At the end of the month, work out what would have happened if you'd staked the same small amount on each tip, and subtract commission from the winners. Most people who try this find the result is a loss, or close to nothing, even for channels that looked impressive in their screenshots. It costs you nothing and gives you real evidence instead of marketing.

Responsible Betting Without Tips

  1. Decide your monthly budget before the month starts, and write it down.
  2. Bet only on markets you fully understand.
  3. Use small, fixed stakes and never raise them to recover losses.
  4. Limit yourself to a few bets per match.
  5. Withdraw winnings instead of letting them build up.
  6. Take regular breaks, especially after a losing run.

These habits won't turn betting into income, but they'll keep it affordable, and that's the most realistic goal for any recreational bettor.

What to Say When Someone Offers You a Tip

Friends and group members often pass on tips with good intentions. A polite way to handle it is to ask two questions: "What odds?" and "How many of your last fifty tips won, including the losers?" Most people can't answer the second question, which says a lot. You don't have to argue; simply decide your own bets based on your own budget and understanding, and let tips remain someone else's guess.
